The finding

Every page on pinkelephants.org.au ships with an empty body.

The site is an Angular app that builds its content in the visitor's browser after the page arrives. Google runs that code, so it can still index you. The AI crawlers that feed ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews mostly do not. What they fetch is about 7KB of scaffolding, a title, a meta description, and nothing else. The resources, the loss types, the helpline, the peer support, all of it is invisible to them.

The test that matters

I searched the way a woman would the night she is told, without using your name: miscarriage support Australia, where to get help after early pregnancy loss.

Not one result was a Pink Elephants page.

The answers belonged to Pregnancy Birth and Baby, Miscarriage Australia, MISS, the Stillbirth CRE and the Perinatal Loss Centre. Pink Elephants appeared twice, both times through somebody else's website: PANDA's page about your helpline, and Healthdirect's partner listing. You are being described by third parties rather than heard directly, and every one of those mentions is a page you do not control and cannot update.

02 / How I sequenced it

Prioritisation logic

I ordered this by dependency and reversibility, not by effort or by what demos well. Four tests, applied in order.

TEST 01

Does everything else depend on it?

Foundations first, even when they are invisible. New pages, campaigns and ad spend all pour into the same unreadable site. Fixing rendering multiplies every future thing you do. Doing it later means redoing the work in between.

TEST 02

Does it multiply what you already own?

You have ten years of resources, the 2022 State of Early Pregnancy Loss report, the Care Standards and Not Just A Loss. Making existing assets legible beats commissioning new ones. Leverage before creation.

TEST 03

Can it be measured inside its own phase?

Anything that cannot be proven within its phase gets scheduled after something that can. This is why week one is instrumentation: without a baseline, every later claim is an opinion.

TEST 04

Could it reach someone in grief before it is right?

Work that touches a woman at her worst moment moves to the slowest lane with the most review. Technical plumbing can ship fast because a mistake is invisible. A wrong routing decision is not.

Applying those four gives an order that is close to the opposite of what is most tempting. The quiz is the most exciting piece and the easiest to show you, and it is deliberately third, because sending more people through a front door they still cannot find is the wrong problem to solve first.

03 / The work

Four phases, sixteen weeks

1
WEEKS 1–5

Make the content machine-readable

  • Baseline instrumentation: GA4 events, referral sources, drop-off on the support paths, current AI answer position for 20 priority questions
  • Server-side rendering with Pixell, so the HTML arrives with the content already in it
  • robots.txt with a deliberate decision on each AI crawler, plus a real sitemap
  • Schema.org markup: Organization, MedicalWebPage, FAQPage, and the helpline as a Service with its actual hours
Why first: it is the cheapest phase and the only one everything else stands on. It adds no new content and no new risk to the community, and it turns ten years of existing writing from invisible into quotable. If we only ever did this phase, it would still be the highest-return work on the list.
2
WEEKS 4–11

One page per question

  • Break the 13 loss types onto their own URLs, each answering the questions actually asked about that loss
  • Rewrite the opening of each page to answer in the first 40 words, because that is the part an AI quotes
  • Publish the 2022 report and Care Standards as readable pages, not only as PDFs behind a form
  • Rebuild the health professional hub around referral, with the GP waiting room flyer and care standards indexable
Why second: AI answers are assembled per passage, not per site. A woman told she has had a molar pregnancy searches those exact words, and right now there is no page on earth with your name on it that is about only that. This is where the non-branded traffic is, and it cannot start before phase 1 or the new pages are born invisible too.
3
WEEKS 7–14

Route people in one step

  • The triage quiz, tested with peer support companions before it goes anywhere near the public
  • Routing into the real services: The Circle, LiveChat, the PANDA helpline, the Facebook communities, Pregnancy Loss Circle
  • Companion matching on the dimensions women actually ask for: same loss type, regional, trying again
  • The data schema and consent design, built with the incoming Head of Data and Insights
Why third: this is the piece that touches a grieving woman directly, so it gets the slowest lane and the most review. It also only pays off once phases 1 and 2 are bringing strangers to the site. Building it first would mean a beautiful front door on a house nobody can find.
4
WEEKS 11–16

Own the name and the clinical pathway

  • Reclaim the brand search: a productivity app called Pink Elephants outranks you on your own name in both app stores
  • Correct and enrich the third-party pages already carrying you: Healthdirect, PANDA, Pregnancy Birth and Baby, SupportConnect
  • Referral integration groundwork with HotDoc and the practice management systems
  • CPD and CPE accreditation pathway, so a GP meets Pink Elephants during training rather than trying to recall it mid-consult
  • Readout against the week one baseline
Why last: every item here depends on somebody else saying yes, and partnership timelines are not ours to control. It also lands far better with three phases of evidence behind it. Walking into Healthdirect or HotDoc with measured reach is a different conversation from walking in with a proposal.

05 / Sam's second question

What this unlocks for data

Today roughly 85,000 people a year arrive, and you learn almost nothing about what any of them needed. Attribution exists only inside Google Ads, and "how did you hear about us" is only asked once someone has already entered a programme, which means the people who bounced are never counted.

SUPPORT

Every routing decision is a structured record: type of loss, how long ago, what was chosen, and just as usefully what was offered and declined. That is a live map of which programmes fit which experience, replacing the assumption that the mix you built over ten years is still the right mix.

ADVOCACY

Unmet need by loss type and by region, updating continuously. The 2022 State of Early Pregnancy Loss report becomes a standing instrument rather than a survey you have to fund and run again. That is what turns a submission into evidence.

AWARENESS

Publishable statistics from a population nobody else reaches: women at the point of loss rather than months later in a clinical sample. The intersectional layer Sam described is real, and it only exists if the questions are asked at the moment of need.

Privacy by design, decided up front

The single most important design decision is one I would make in week one and never revisit: the routing system collects need, not identity. It does not need a name, a date of birth or a diagnosis to send someone to the right resource. If we hold need separately from identity from the very first record, the dataset is publishable, shareable with researchers, and structurally incapable of becoming the kind of breach that ends charities.

Retrofitting that later is close to impossible, which is why it belongs in phase 3 alongside the schema rather than in a compliance review afterwards. It also keeps the system out of health record territory deliberately: the quiz routes, it does not diagnose, and that distinction should be defended as a design constraint rather than discovered as a legal one.

06 / Honest risks

What could go wrong

Pixell is on the critical path

Phase 1 needs changes to a site an external agency builds and hosts. If server-side rendering is not something they can schedule quickly, the bridge is a prerender layer that serves crawlers a fully rendered copy of the same page. It buys the result in about two weeks. I would be straight with you that it is a bridge and not the destination: Google treats dynamic rendering as a workaround rather than good practice, and we should still land proper rendering. This is the first thing I would want to ask Pixell about, ideally in week one.

Being found is not the same as being ready

If this works, more women arrive, and they arrive at the acute end. LiveChat is mid-migration to Freshdesk and peer support companions are volunteers. Capacity needs to be part of the phase 3 conversation, not a surprise in phase 4. I would rather stage the traffic and know the support holds than hit a number and fail the people behind it.

The AI layer can misquote you

Making content machine-readable means models will paraphrase it, including the clinical parts. Structured markup plus unambiguous phrasing on the medical pages reduces that, and the emergency guidance on ectopic pregnancy needs to survive being summarised. This argues for doing the markup properly rather than avoiding the whole exercise, but it is a real risk and it belongs on the register.

07 / Over to you

What I could not see from outside

  1. Pixell's contract. Is server-side rendering within scope, and what is their turnaround on a change of that size?
  2. Analytics access. Is GA4 running behind the tag manager, and how far back does the history go? The baseline is only as good as what already exists.
  3. Salesforce. What is already captured about how people arrive, and where would routing data naturally land?
  4. The Freshdesk migration. When does it complete? Phase 3 routing should point at the destination, not the thing being replaced.
  5. Google Ads spend. Some of it is likely buying traffic that better organic and AI presence would deliver free, which would fund the rest of this.
  6. The Head of Data and Insights. When do they start? The phase 3 schema is genuinely better designed with them in the room than handed to them afterwards.
